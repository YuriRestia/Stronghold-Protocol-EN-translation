# Gameplay guide

> English translation of [PLAYING.md](PLAYING.md). The Chinese original is the authoritative version. Game terms follow the official EN client (as used by the in-game English UI, `public/i18n/en.json`).

*Stronghold Protocol: Alliance* is auto-chess + tower defense: during the Rest Phase you recruit Operators at the Dispatch Center, arrange your formation and assign Equipment; during the Battle Phase your Operators deploy automatically and hold off the enemies pouring out of the red gates, and every enemy that gets through costs you Life Points (LP). In Team Simulation, 1–4 players **cooperate** (there is no PvP); survive to round 14 and defeat the Enemy Leader to win.

The numbers in this document come from the official data tables (`data/config.json`, etc.) and the [research](research/00-INDEX.md); in game, 📖 "How to Play" in the bottom-left corner has the official tutorial images (downloaded with the assets; if they weren't downloaded, bullet-point text is shown instead). For controls, see [README · Controls](../README.en.md#controls); keyboard shortcuts and how to change them are in [§11](#11-hotkeys).

Interface language: Chinese by default; switch between **中文 / English / 日本語 / 한국어 / 繁體中文** at the top right of the title screen or in "Settings" (the choice is remembered, no refresh needed). The Japanese, Korean and Traditional Chinese game texts come from the official JP / KR / TW server data, but the interface text isn't translated yet — Japanese and Korean show the English interface, Traditional Chinese shows the Simplified Chinese interface; community contributions are welcome (docs/I18N.md, "Adding a language"). The English game texts for Operators, skills, Alliances, Equipment, enemies, etc. come from the official EN server data, and all interface text has English; after switching to English, the only things still shown in Chinese are player names (bots' names too), the rewritten "next two battles" description of multi-round bounties, and game texts the official EN server doesn't have yet (some Custom Squad Operators and newer Modules); the tutorial images in "How to Play" are the official Chinese images, with English titles (docs/I18N.md).

Operator voices (official lines): "Operator Voices" in "Settings" sets the volume, and "Voice Language" chooses **中文 / 日本語** (Chinese by default, independent of the interface language, and remembered; where a Japanese line is missing, that line plays in Chinese). This branch also has **English / 한국어** (English by default; the names of the dubs follow the interface language, e.g. Chinese / Japanese / English / Korean in the English interface; when an Operator doesn't have that dub it falls back to English, then Japanese, then Chinese); with the "Native language" switch next to it turned on, Operators who have a native-language dub (Italian, Russian, etc.) speak in their native language, and turning it off goes back to the chosen voice language; in the details of Operator Loadout and Custom Squad Operators you can choose a different dub for a single Operator (Chinese / Japanese / English / Korean, plus the native-language dub that Operator has), and tapping one plays a sample line; changing "Voice Language" in "Settings" clears these individual choices. Selecting an Operator — an Operator in the battle area or on the Bench, or an Operator card in the shop or a Promotion Reward — plays an official "Operator selected" line, in the Rest Phase too; when you select several in a row the new line takes over the one being spoken, but it doesn't interrupt battle voices such as deployment and skills that are playing. Deployment (the first Operator deployed in each battle says "Mission start"), skill, engage and settlement voices only play during battle. Stand-in and prototype Operators (Reserve Operators, Sharp, Mechanist, etc.), Alliance/Supportive Operators and summons have no official voices and make no sound when selected.

## 1. Flow of a match

```
Confirm Match Info (Team: 25 s) → Choose Strategy (draft) → Execute Protocol
→ each round: [Improv] → Rest Phase → Battle → [Unite] → Settlement
→ round 14: Final Assault → (if conditions are met) round 15: Hidden Core → result titles
```

| Phase | Description |
|---|---|
| Confirm Match Info | The match's map (Standard Simulation is always "Battlefield #01"; Perilous picks randomly from 8 maps, Dire / Ultimate randomly from the 7 that exclude Battlefield #01), the enemy faction (three training enemy types such as Refraction and Flying: as in the official game, each takes 3 of the match's 15 round slots, and the round-14 Leader fight and the round-15 Hidden Core take one slot each, so on Perilous / Dire / Ultimate 7–9 of the waves in rounds 1–13 are these three types, at most 3 waves each, and the remaining waves are of the "Special" type; each wave has only one type), **the disabled Alliances and their Operators**, and the Leader preview. Continues once everyone has confirmed; at most 25 seconds in Team Simulation; untimed when you are the only player (Solo Simulation, or Team Simulation with only AI teammates). |
| Choose Strategy | The 40 Strategies (squad leaders) determine your **starting LP (20–45)** and one permanent rule. Team: players pick in turn in a random order, **30 seconds each**; the top right and "Deciding" show the same countdown; AI teammates pick instantly; a Strategy a teammate has taken can't be picked again; each player can **skip once** (moving to the end of the order); the host can turn on "**AI Teammates Pick Last**" in the alliance room (off by default): in the Strategy draft and the Improv picks every Doctor then goes before the AI teammates (the order among Doctors and among AIs is still random, and Doctors on AI Autopilot or disconnected still count as Doctors), and a skip moves you after the other Doctors who haven't picked yet but before the AIs (to the end if there are no other Doctors); on timeout your **currently selected** Strategy is picked automatically (Warfarin if a teammate already took it). Solo: free choice, untimed; Team Simulation with you as the only player is also untimed. While choosing you can click "View disabled Alliances & Operators" under the pick order on the left to review the Alliances from "Confirm Match Info" (greyed out, number disabled) and the Operators disabled this match: view-only — the countdown keeps running, and the bottom shows whose turn it is and the seconds left; close it with "Close", by clicking outside the window or with Esc; it closes automatically when it's the next player's turn. |
| Improv | Appears before some rounds: in Team, 6 cards in total and players take 1 each in turn (in random order, with Doctors first when "AI Teammates Pick Last" is on; 30 seconds for the first, 16 for the rest; untimed when you are the only player); in Solo, pick 1 of 3. Cards include Equipment, Funds, Operators, Alliance Stacks, Bounties, etc. **Bounty Decision** adds Bounty enemies to your battles, with the duration marked in blue on the card: "next battle" / "next two battles" bounties appear in one or two battles only and then no more; the official "multi-round bounties" (original text: "every battle after this") are also treated as "next two battles" here and appear in only two battles (e.g. Pointing Stick's Arts Master A2 · multi-round tactical training). The Bounty Decisions of each round follow official match screenshots: round 3 offers 6 "next two battles" bounties (3 costing 1 Fund, 2 costing 2 Funds, 1 costing 3 Funds); round 9 offers each boss's "next battle" bounty and "Originium Slug/Training"; round 11 on Dire / Ultimate is usually a Bounty Decision (it may also be a Secret Shop or Tactical Decision), whose bounties usually include 1 special giant beast, the rest being "next battle" bounties from each faction. Whoever knocks out a Bounty enemy (teammates during Unite count too) gets the Funds; the effects bar on the right shows "N battles left". All 6 pieces of Equipment in the **Secret Shop** are free, and, as in the official game, two identical pieces may appear (each counts as a separate card and can be taken separately); the round-11 (Dire / Ultimate) Secret Shop follows the official game: 2 tier-VI pieces, at least 1 tier-V piece and 1 Alliance Coin, while in earlier Secret Shops on Standard / Perilous any tier may appear. **Tactical Decision** can also offer two identical cards as in the official game (two "Supply" cards have been seen in official screenshots; each counts as a separate card and can be taken separately, each takes effect only once, and if two players each pick a team-wide card the effects stack); the round-11 (Dire / Ultimate) Tactical Decision, per official screenshots, only offers buff cards such as Vow, Reinforce, Equip / Wealth / Supply / Prepare / Promotion, and no enemy-weakening or battlefield-evolution cards. Each card shows its icon and name at the top left with the full effect below (the same official description as the detail card, also shown on phones); cards already taken are dimmed, with the avatar of the player who took it in the top-right corner (hover to see the name). Picking a card takes **two taps**: the first only selects it (gold frame + "Confirm · Tap again"); tapping the same card again or the "Confirm" at the top right makes it take effect; tap another card to switch, or press Esc / tap empty space to cancel. |
| Rest Phase | Buy, arrange your formation, assign Equipment, Freeze / Refresh, upgrade the Dispatch Center; Team has a countdown (65–215 seconds, increasing with the round); Solo Simulation and Team Simulation where you are the only player (with only AI teammates) are untimed. When everyone is "Ready" the battle starts early; you can cancel before everyone is ready; clicking "Ready" collapses the shop automatically and enlarges the board to the whole field, and cancelling Ready expands the shop again ("Collapse" next to your Funds and the shop tab "Expand Shop" toggle it by hand at any time). |
- **Tap a special terrain tile for its description**: tapping **Active Originium**, **Swamp**, **Exhaust Grille**, **Deep Water**, or a **red gate / blue gate / teleport point** on the battlefield pops up its own description card — name, mechanics, and **this stage's own numbers** (e.g. the Active Originium on Battlefield #04: 70 true damage per second, ATK +20%, ASPD +20, lasting 300 seconds). Works in both the Rest Phase and battle. Plain ground / road / ranged tiles show nothing, so a tap there still just "deselects and closes the card".
- **Tap a field device for its description too**: **Barricades** (they block the path: ground enemies walk around them and only smash through when there is no other way; in battle the card shows its live HP, and once destroyed it can't be tapped), **"Double-Eyelid" turrets** (automatic arts attacks that apply Fragile, strengthened by the Alliance with the most Stacks; turrets not yet enabled by Mechanical Aid or an Improv card aren't shown and can't be tapped), **Ranged Tiles** (raised tiles ranged Operators can deploy on) and **Originium-flow blowers** (the wind direction, and the change to Operators' ATK and enemies' movement speed) also pop up a description card when tapped; the numbers all come from the device data of that stage, and device names show the official translation for the current language; boxes removed by an Improv card aren't shown either. Works in both the Rest Phase and battle (GitHub #228, PR #229, thanks to @rickylxw).
| Battle | Each player fights on their own battlefield (the battle is simulated in your browser, at 2× speed). Operators deploy automatically **column by column from left to right, each column top to bottom** (the official "top to bottom > left to right": the leftmost column first) and spend DP (starting at 10, +1/sec). The time limit is 45–115 seconds (real seconds, i.e. the countdown on screen); enemies still alive when time runs out count as leaks. The capsule in the middle of the top bar is the official "resolved / enemies this round": killing one and leaking one both count as resolved (starting at 0/3, leak one 1/3, kill one 2/3, kill the one that splits 3/3); enemies that split off, summons and enemies summoned by a Leader count in neither the numerator nor the denominator, but each one that leaks still costs LP and also breaks a perfect battle (all 4 Grindstones killed and the 8 that split off all leaking into the blue gate: capsule 4/4, 8 LP lost), so the capsule also fills up in Leader stages with summons. When an enemy enters the blue gate, the LP in the top bar **immediately** shows the value after the deduction (red text + "−N", at most 10 per round), finalized at Settlement; during the Unite phase it is marked "Uniting", and the part teammates intercept is not deducted in the end. The team list on the left shows teammates' pending LP loss the same way. A knocked-out Operator **stays on the tile where it fell** (lying down, slightly greyed) and redeploys on that same tile later (as in the official game; one that falls on another Operator's or a summon's starting position goes back to lie down at its own starting position, even if the summon has disappeared; the one exception is Ulpianus: when he is knocked out while his S3 has moved him to the anchor, he goes back to lie down and redeploy at his own deployment position — an intentional change the project maintainer made on 2026-10-07 after community feedback, different from the official "fall in place"); Operators forced off the field by their own effects (the end of Surtr's "Remnant Ash", the end of Nearl the Radiant Knight's S2, etc.) likewise fall in place and redeploy in place; the tile of a downed Operator **is not taken by anything else** — Raid Alliance redeploys, reinforcements and summons all avoid it. The circle over its head is the redeploy countdown: a green ring + seconds; it turns into a yellow "DP" when there isn't enough DP. The row under the HP bar is **Elemental Damage** (as in the original: element icon + white bar): Operators get a colored disc with an icon and enemies a small solid disc; the white bar on the right is the remaining element value and decreases right-to-left like an HP bar, and the moment it runs out is the burst (when a large chunk of Elemental Damage lands at once the white bar drops by a chunk too — e.g. each hit of a Skimming Sea Drifter is about 25%–50%); after bursting comes the burst cooldown (Burn / Nervous Impairment / Corrosion 10 seconds, enemy Corrosion 8 seconds, Necrosis 15 seconds), during which the bar turns the **element's color** (Nervous Impairment teal, Corrosion blue, Burn orange-red, Necrosis grey-purple), slowly refills over the cooldown and the icon flashes — a colored bar means the unit has already burst and is under the burst effect, not the element value remaining; during this time no element accumulates and none can be healed, and it resets to zero afterwards. Elemental bursts deal **source-less damage** that doesn't benefit from the Operator's damage buffs, penetration or Equipment bonuses (e.g. Underflow's Burn burst against Seaborn is a fixed 7000), but the kill still goes to the Operator who triggered the burst; Elemental Fragility only increases elemental damage and doesn't speed up the element bar; Fragile doesn't affect elemental damage. Enemies already killed can't receive further Elemental Damage. |
| Unite | See section 5. |
| Settlement | Deduct LP, pay out Bounty Funds, settle Stacks; players with LP ≤ 0 are eliminated (their Operators go back to the shared pool). When Settlement starts, the official "**Battle over**" box pops up in the middle of the screen (it disappears by itself after about 3 seconds and doesn't block input), with one line below it giving only **your own** LP lost this round: "**All unharmed!**" if nothing was lost, "**LP reduced by N**" if some was; the official battle-end sound plays at the same time (one each for: LP lost, nothing lost thanks to Unite, nothing lost in a normal round). For Unite rounds see section 5. The Final Assault and the Hidden Core have no such box (they go straight to the result titles or the Hidden Core); eliminated players and spectators don't get the box. |

**Disconnecting and leaving**: in Team Simulation, reopen the page within 10 minutes of a disconnect to return to your seat; meanwhile your formation fights automatically as usual, and Improv and the Rest Phase pick / ready up automatically when time runs out, but nothing is bought for you (to have the AI play for you, choose "Step Away (AI Autopilot)" under "Leave Simulation"; AI Autopilot won't destroy your items to make room — when the Bench is full it sells benched Operators to make space for items). In Solo Simulation the Rest Phase and Improv are untimed; after a disconnect or closing the page, open it in the same browser **within 24 hours** to continue; Solo Simulation battles (including Final Assault / Hidden Core) can also be paused at any time with "Pause" in the top bar (or Space), which stops the countdown and the Leader-fight overtime LP drain together (if you disconnect while paused it resumes automatically and the server finishes the battle). Team Simulation battles cannot be paused. "Abandon Simulation": in Solo Simulation it ends the match immediately; in Team Simulation it counts as you being eliminated (your Operators go back to the shared pool) and your teammates carry on. Restarting the server ends every match.

**Rounds and Improv rounds**

| Difficulty | Team: rounds / Improv rounds | Solo: rounds / Improv rounds |
|---|---|---|
| Standard Simulation | 14 / 3, 9 | 9 (round 9 is the Leader) / none |
| Perilous Simulation | 14 (+15) / 3, 6, 9 | 14 (+15) / 6, 9 |
| Dire Simulation · Ultimate Simulation | 14 (+15) / 3, 9, 11 | 14 (+15) / 3, 9, 11 |

The higher the difficulty, the higher the enemies' ATK / HP multipliers, the more dangerous enemies appear, and the more HP the Leader has.

| Leftover Funds | **Reset to zero at the end of the Rest Phase** (except with the Strategy "Cannot"); clicking "Ready" (or pressing Space) while you still have Funds asks for confirmation once first |
| Recruit price (by tier I–VI) | 2 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 4 / 4 |
| Refresh | 1 Fund |
| Selling an Operator | Any Operator (including Elites) returns only **1** Fund |
| Equipment / Arts | The Dispatch Center sells all Equipment except special Equipment: the 4 special Victorian Hammers (Tremble / Durable / Accelerated / Burn) come only from the Victoria Alliance (every 25 Stacks) and Rockrock's custom items, and Mutated Cells come only from Quintus's Strategy. Equipment can't be sold; on the Bench it can only be "Destroyed" (no refund). Assigned Equipment is locked to its Operator: it can only be replaced by a third piece (a replace dialog pops up; choose the piece to replace and it is destroyed; items destroyed on equipping, such as the Alliance Coin and the Doctor Hologram, work the same way: the selected piece is destroyed first and then the item takes effect, leaving the Operator an empty slot — Elite Operators can't use the Doctor Hologram, and then nothing is destroyed), or returned to the Bench when the Operator is sold / merged into an Elite. **Mutated Cells** never run out: after the battle the holder is destroyed (if deployed, its tile is freed and the remaining deployable count goes up by 1), the Mutated Cells and its other Equipment first return to the Bench and can be assigned to any Operator again; then you get a random Normal Operator of the next tier up (max VI) (even if the holder was an Elite), which goes to the Bench like any other newly obtained Operator (to the Temporary Bench if full) and must be redeployed by you; if it completes a set of copies needed for a merge, it merges into an Elite as usual. The **Durable Victorian Hammer** locks HP for 8 seconds on the first lethal damage, **once per deployment**: after a knockout and redeploy (or a Raid jump redeploy, or a revive by the M3 Cocoon Shell / Ermengarde) it can lock again, and the same goes for hammers lent by the Narantuya Strategy; a lock in progress when a loan expires lasts the full 8 seconds; being Devoured at the start while standing in front of an Ægir Operator, or HP drain from its own Originium Solvent, also uses up that one lock. The **Pointing Stick** (one every 2 rounds from Dobermann's Strategy) is destroyed after being dragged onto the field and used, and pops up 3 tactical training bounties during the Rest Phase (only you can see them; after a page refresh or reconnect they are still the same 3): tap one and tap again to confirm, and the chosen one becomes your next bounty; you can't click "Ready" until you've chosen. If you still haven't chosen when the Team Rest Phase countdown ends, one is picked at random; in Solo Simulation and with only one player the Rest Phase is untimed, so nothing is picked automatically. AI teammates and seats on AI Autopilot choose by their bounty score. The tactical training reward is only paid for "a perfect battle in your own action phase" (in the Final Assault and the Hidden Core: no leaks on your side and the team wins); 'Mystery Customer' still gives one random bounty |
| Freeze | Free; keeps all currently unbought slots into the next round; you can still Refresh manually while frozen. With the Strategy "Orchid", every manual Refresh freezes the same-name Operator card it brings on its own: only that card shows frost (the Freeze button above doesn't light up); if you don't Refresh again it stays into the next round, and another manual Refresh redraws it along with the rest |
| Upgrading the Dispatch Center (1→2 … 5→6) | Perilous and above 5 / 8 / 11 / 12 / 13; Team Standard 5 / 8 / 10 / 11 / 11; Solo Standard 1 / 1 / 5 / 8 / 10. **The price drops by 1 each round** (minimum 0) and is recalculated from the next level's price after upgrading. Upgrading to a level with more Operator slots (Perilous and above: 4 at level 2, 5 at level 4; Standard: 4 at level 2, 5 at level 6) makes **the new slot appear immediately, but empty** (as in the official game: upgrading only opens the slot, and Operators appear in the new slot at the next refresh — a manual Refresh or the automatic one at round start; an empty slot is drawn as an empty frame, without "Recruited"); the existing cards stay as they are |
| Other income | Bounty enemies, the "Foresight" and "Marvel" Alliances, the "Knightly Savings" Equipment, some Strategies and Attributes |

The Dispatch Center level determines the **highest tier** that can appear in the shop and the number of slots (Perilous and above: 3 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 5 Operator slots + 1 Equipment slot).

## 3. Recruiting, the Bench and promotion

- **Buying takes two taps**: the first tap on a shop card only selects it (the card lifts and opens its details, and "Confirm Purchase · Tap again" appears at the bottom); tapping **anywhere** on the card again (including the gold confirm bar) buys it; tap elsewhere or press Esc to cancel. Upgrading the Dispatch Center and Promotion Rewards also take two taps; free picks from Strategies or Equipment (such as the 3 pieces of Equipment refreshed after Catherine's 【Targeted Drop】 upgrade, the 2 pieces of Equipment from Narantuya's 【Passing Down the Profit】, or the 3 Operators from the Dispatch Module) also appear in the shop card slots, with their source written in the title on the left; Equipment is shown as Equipment cards (icon, name, description, FREE); pick 1 to get it for free. When several free picks are queued they appear one after another, and the title and the hint button after "Later" say how many remain (e.g. "+1"). When you can't buy (e.g. you're already Ready), tapping a card only opens its details.
- **Shared pool** (Team): each Operator has a limited number of copies — 12 / 14 / 18 / 16 / 8 / 5 for tiers I–VI — shared by all players; Operators you hold (board + Bench) use up copies, while those displayed in the shop don't. Once everyone has taken all copies of an Operator it can't be rolled any more; eliminated players' Operators return to the pool.
- **Slot limits**: at most **8** deployed on the board; **10** Bench slots. When the Bench is full you **can't buy or claim** any Operator or Equipment (including the card that would complete 3 copies for an Elite merge, as in the official game) — free a slot first by selling, deploying or assigning; newly obtained units (merged Elites, rewards, returned Equipment, etc.) go to the **Temporary Bench** (the 5 slots in front of the Bench), and as soon as a Bench slot frees up, units on the Temporary Bench **move into it automatically** (right to left). While the Temporary Bench holds units you **can't Ready**; the board marks that row with a red dashed frame and explains the rule: move them to the Bench or battlefield, assign or use them, or sell / destroy them. Units that enter the Temporary Bench during a Rest Phase are handled automatically if still there at **the end of that Rest Phase** (Operators return to the pool, Equipment is destroyed); units that enter during battle, Settlement or after you're Ready (e.g. battle rewards, Elites merged at Settlement) are kept until the end of the **next Rest Phase**, so you always get a chance to deal with them.
- **Elite promotion**: 3 copies of the same Operator (deployed or not) automatically merge into an **Elite** (big boosts to level, skills and Module; Kazemaru needs only 2), and Elites don't merge further. If any of the merged Operators is **already deployed on the battlefield**, the Elite appears directly **in that Operator's position** (keeping the original facing and not taking an extra deployment slot; if two or three are on the field, the one deployed first — leftmost column first, top to bottom within a column — is used); otherwise the Elite goes to the Bench (the Temporary Bench if full). All previously assigned Equipment returns to the Bench and must be reassigned; the merged Operators' summons are removed, and an Elite on the field sends its own summons to the Bench. When you select a shop card that would complete a merge, the tile where the Elite will appear lights up with a gold border, and the detail card that opens alongside states where it goes ("Promotable: the Elite Operator will appear at the original position in the battle area / will go to the Bench"; the detail card docks automatically on the side that doesn't cover that tile). Merges completed at round start or Settlement through Strategies, rewards, etc. also play the gold promotion effect when the Elite appears.
- **Promotion Reward**: each promotion grants one Special Recruit: pick 1 **for free** from 3 **distinct** random Operators of the **next tier up** (max VI) (if fewer than 3 Operators remain at that tier, the rest are filled from the tier below). It can't be Refreshed or Frozen, and expires at the end of the round.
- **Operator Loadout (skills, Modules, Potential and Development)**: the "Operator Loadout" button in the lobby, the room and "Confirm Match Info" opens a screen with three tabs: "Operator Loadout", "Operator Roster" and "Custom Squad". "Operator Loadout" lists the Operators as a table, one row per Operator, with columns for the **skill**, the Elite **Module** and **Potential · Development**: just tap the skill icon or Module icon in the row ("None" comes first; the Module only applies to the Elite) or change the two drop-downs; tap the avatar or name to open the details on the right: Potential and Development, trait, skills (switchable between the Normal Lv.4 / Elite Lv.7 values), In-Match Stats and Module (on narrow screens — under 1000 pixels wide, e.g. a phone in landscape — the details cover the list; tap "Operator List" to go back). **Potential** (1–6) and **Development** (Non-Elite / Elite 1 / Elite 2 / Elite 2 Lv.60) are set per Operator and shared by its Normal, Elite and Custom Squad versions; the default is Potential 6 and Elite 2 Lv.60 (the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-08: max Potential and full bonus by default). Official rules: Potential is the Potential of your own copy of that Operator; Development is the **owned-Operator bonus** from the "Bonus" section of the dispatch manual — Non-Elite +0%, Elite 1 ATK and DEF +5%, Elite 2 ATK +10% and DEF and HP +5%, Elite 2 Lv.60 all three +10% — multiplied separately, not added to other "ATK +x%" bonuses; officially an unowned privileged Operator uses Potential 1 and no bonus, so to play by the official rules set it to Potential 1 and Non-Elite. Stand-in Operators and Custom Squad prototypes have no Potential or Development, and the settings don't apply to them. Shop cards, detail cards and "In-Match Stats" all follow your settings; in Team Simulation your teammates' Operators follow their settings. Like skills and Modules this is an **out-of-match setting**: changes made before "Confirm Match Info" ends apply to this match, and later ones are only saved for the next match. "Reset to Default" and "Reset All to Default" reset Potential and Development too, "Export / Import" includes them, and the number on the "Operator Loadout" button also counts Operators whose Potential or Development was changed.
- **Operator Roster (stand-ins)**: the second tab of the same screen, "**Operator Roster**", lets you **remove** Operators you don't own (tap a card to toggle "Owned / Not owned"). As in the official game only the 55 "normal" pieces can be removed (the screen lists the 53 in this season's shop by tier, each saying who stands in for it and with which skill); the 74 "preset" pieces (mostly common tier 1–2 Operators) always field the Operator themselves, owned or not, and aren't listed. Everything is owned by default. A removed Operator is **shown as its stand-in**, as in the official game: shop and Promotion Reward cards, units on the Bench and Temporary Bench, the battle area and the lineup on the result screen all show the stand-in's avatar, name and model (e.g. SilverAsh shows as Sharp), with a small "Stand-in" mark; the detail card shows the stand-in's portrait, name, skill, talents and Module, with "SilverAsh's stand-in" under the name. **Alliances, Attribute, tier, price and merging still follow the original Operator** (the Alliances and Attribute on the cards and detail cards are the original's), while in battle the stats, skill, talents, Module, attack range and melee / ranged position are the stand-in's (e.g. SilverAsh → Sharp's S2 "Drawing Sword"). The same stand-in may stand in for several Operators (Sharp stands in for Vulpisfoglia, SilverAsh, Entelechia, etc.), and they merge separately by their original Operators: tell them apart by tier and Alliances, and open the detail card to see who it stands in for. In Team Simulation, teammates viewing your Bench and battle area, or your Alliance members, also see the stand-ins. A stand-in's skill and Module are fixed by the piece, and "Operator Loadout" doesn't apply to it; stand-ins have no summons (remove Muelsyse and there are no Flowing Shapes). This is an **out-of-match setting**: it applies from the start of the next match, and changes during a match are only saved for the next one; in Team Simulation it only affects your own pieces, and teammates' pieces follow their settings. There is no official friend-support borrowing: to field the Operator themselves, set it back to "Owned". "Own All" restores everything in one click, "Export / Import" carries the setting to another browser, and the "Operator Loadout" button in the lobby shows "Stand-in N" to remind you how many Operators are removed.
- **Custom Squad**: the third tab of the same screen, "**Custom Squad**", has 4 slots: two tier-5 and two tier-6. Tap a slot to open the selection list; each slot takes either a **6★ Operator you own** (one not in the piece pool, marked "Owned": choose any of the 3 skills and, for the Elite, any Module or none; Modules that only work in Integrated Strategies or Reclamation Algorithm can't be chosen: ISW-A, SO-A / SO-B, RA-A) or a **prototype Operator** (marked "Prototype": 9 6★ such as Sharp and Mechanist, plus 6 4★ Reserve Operators for tier 5; skill and Module are the same as when standing in and can't be changed). The same prototype can be in a tier-5 and a tier-6 slot at once; an Operator you own can only take one slot; the two slots of one tier can't hold the same Operator. The list only shows Operators whose battle logic is done (all prototypes are available, and owned 6★ are added as their battle logic is completed). In a match, Custom Squad Operators **only appear in your own shop**: tier-5 slot Operators only roll once the Dispatch Center reaches level 5, and tier-6 ones at level 6; the roll chance is the same as other Operators of that tier, each slot has its own stock (8 copies for tier 5, 5 for tier 6, not shared with teammates), the price is the same as other Operators of that tier, and they can also appear in Promotion Rewards; effects, rewards or Improv cards that "grant a random Operator" can also draw your own Custom Squad Operators (by that effect's own tier rules, regardless of the Dispatch Center level). - Each round the shop automatically refreshes the slots that aren't frozen.
- **"First manual Refresh this round" is counted per Operator** (Lappland): it applies to the first manual Refresh she encounters this round, even on the Bench — Refresh first, then buy Lappland, and the next Refresh still stacks for her; an Elite newly merged this round counts as a new Lappland — even if the Lapplands merged into it already triggered this round, the Elite still gets +8 on the next manual Refresh this round.

## 4. Formation and facing

- Drag Operators from the Bench onto the board: melee / ranged tiles are highlighted as valid spots. **Melee Operators whose trait says "can be deployed on ranged tiles" (Hookmasters and Push Strokers: Gladiia, Cliffheart, Enforcer, and Weedy from Custom Squad) can also go on ranged tiles**, Normal and Elite alike, with any Module or none; other melee Operators can only go on ground tiles. On a ranged tile they attack as usual but don't block enemies. **No deploying in water**: the pool of water down the middle of Battlefield #08 (Tide Controller) (Deep Water) refuses deployment just like the official game, so no Operator or summon can be placed there; Raid Alliance redeploys and Tacticians' reinforcements auto-picking a tactical point also only land on shore. After you release, the **direction wheel** appears: swipe up / right / down / left, the board previews the rotated attack range with orange stripes, and release to confirm; release in the center or tap "✕ Tap to cancel" to cancel.
- To change only the facing: drag the Operator back onto its own tile and choose a direction. Facing is kept into the next round.
- **Selection works by board tile**: tapping a tile selects the unit standing on that tile, and tapping an empty tile selects nothing (tapping the head of the Operator drawn on the tile behind counts as tapping that tile behind). During battle, allies are also selected by tile; enemies move between tiles, so tap their body or near their feet (within 0.6 tiles); for flying enemies, tap their body.
- While dragging, **the tile under your pointer / finger** is where it lands and is highlighted. When that tile can take it (a board or Bench tile), the Operator's model stands right on that tile, as in the official deployment: moving your finger within a tile doesn't move the model, and moving onto the next tile that can take it makes it stand there; when the pointer isn't over a tile that can take it (e.g. over the shop bar), the model follows under your pointer / finger (feet about half a tile below the pointer). Mouse and touch work the same. Equipment dragged to a tile is assigned to the Operator on that tile; releasing over the shop bar puts it back where it was.
- Tapping an Operator shows its attack range and the bottom buttons ("Retreat" back to the Bench, "Sell +1"); right-click / long-press to view details. During battle and when viewing a teammate's field, opening the detail card of one of our Operators or summons on the field (a teammate's included) also draws its **current attack range** on the board (from its current position, facing and skills — e.g. the enlarged range while a range-expanding skill is on); it disappears when you close the detail card, drag, or choose a direction; enemies and units on the Bench and Temporary Bench aren't drawn. In the detail card: next to the portrait are its Alliances, and the first item below is the Operator's **Attribute** (e.g. "At the end of the Rest Phase …"), followed by trait, stats, attack range, skills, Module, Equipment and talents. Stats are **live values**: during battle they update 4 times per second (current HP, ATK, DEF, RES, Attack Interval, Block); during the Rest Phase they show the board Operator's values **at battle start** (with Equipment, Alliance Stacks, Attributes, Strategy and Improv effects included); green when above base and red when below, with the difference shown (a shorter Attack Interval counts as a buff); hover to see the base value. The base value is the official data at that tier's level, skill level (and the Elite's Module), with the **Potential and Development** you set for that Operator in "Operator Loadout" (default Potential 6, Elite 2 Lv.60) and 0 Trust: the Potential bonuses include deployment cost, redeploy time, ATK / HP / DEF / RES / ASPD and "talent enhanced", and summons' talents use the summoner's Potential; Development multiplies ATK, DEF and max HP separately by the owned-Operator bonus (Elite 2 Lv.60 ×1.1; summons aren't multiplied). Stand-in and prototype Operators have no Potential tiers and no owned-Operator bonus (0.2.2; in 0.2.1 every Operator had max Potential and no owned-Operator bonus, GitHub #252).
- **Summons are placed by hand**: after an Operator with deployable summons is put on the board, its summons go to the Bench (Silence's Medic Drone, Shamare's Cursed Doll, Catherine's 2 support devices, Skadi the Corrupting Heart's Seaborn, Vigil's Wolfpack, Muelsyse's Flowing Shapes; summons of the same Operator stack in one slot and don't take a deployment slot), and are dragged onto the board and given a facing like Operators. Catherine's support device gives a Barrier to the Operator on **the tile it faces**, so place it next to an Operator, facing it. **Tacticians' reinforcements** (Vigil's Wolfpack, Muelsyse's Flowing Shapes — i.e. picking the tactical point) can, as in the official game, **only be placed within the summoner's attack range**: only those tiles light up while dragging; if you later change the Tactician's facing in place, reinforcements outside the new attack range return to the Bench (those still in range stay put; if both the Bench and Temporary Bench are full and the reinforcements have nowhere to go, the facing change doesn't apply and "Bench is full" is shown). Retreating or selling an Operator removes its summons with it; **moving an Operator to another tile (including dragging a summon onto the Operator to swap places) returns its placed summons to the Bench**, and they must be placed again. When the Bench is full, returned summons go to the Temporary Bench, and any not handled by the end of the Rest Phase return to the Bench next round.
- In battle: Seaborn, Wolfpack, Flowing Shapes and support devices deploy at their placed positions at battle start; the Medic Drone and Cursed Doll are also deployed once for free at their placed positions at battle start (lasting 10 / 15 seconds; as in the official game no skill activation is needed first), and afterwards reappear at the same spot each time their Operator activates its skill; when the owning Operator is knocked out and redeploys, if the one granted by the skill hasn't appeared yet, it appears immediately. When Shamare leaves the field the Cursed Doll retreats with her; when Silence leaves the field, a Medic Drone already out is unaffected. Summons like the Medic Drone, Cursed Doll and Seaborn (and the Custom Squad Operators' Industrial Water Cannon, Sandbeast, typewriter and Instinct's summons) that can't be attacked and leave when their time is up take no damage of any kind (Active Originium included) and can't be healed; their HP bar is the countdown: it drains over time, and they leave when it's empty. The Champagne Bomb of Swire the Elegant Wit's S2 also doesn't lose HP (not to Active Originium or any damage), can't be healed, and never disappears because of HP; it only disappears after its "welcome gift" blasts the first ground enemy that touches it (the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-08); invisible enemies (neither revealed nor blocked) and sleeping enemies can't be targeted and don't trigger it. Medic Drones, Cursed Dolls, support devices and Seaborn don't appear unless placed; Wolfpack and Flowing Shapes (Tacticians' reinforcements) appear at the tactical point if not placed. After the Wolfpack's last wolf shadow is knocked out it goes into tactical-point form: it temporarily leaves the fight (doesn't block, attack or get attacked, but still occupies its tile), and after the wolf shadows' recovery time (25 seconds) comes back in place at full HP with 1 wolf shadow, then starts adding 1 wolf shadow every 25 seconds again; when Vigil leaves the field the Wolfpack retreats with him, and when Vigil redeploys the Wolfpack reappears with 2 wolf shadows.
- **Blocking is by contact**: an Operator blocks enemies within about 0.7 tiles of its center that are in contact with it (ground Operators can't block flying or low-altitude hovering enemies). When an enemy is wedged between two Operators and the one blocking it falls, retreats or is stunned, another Operator still in contact with it with spare Block **immediately takes over blocking**; the enemy only walks off when nobody has a free slot. Frozen or stunned enemies are blocked by Operators in contact with them too: an Operator redeployed next to one blocks it, and an invisible one is revealed with it (sleeping, levitated and feared enemies still can't be blocked). Enemies walking head-on stop in front of the Operator's tile. **If it's blocked, it can be hit**: any Operator (including ranged Operators and summons on melee tiles) can always attack the enemies it blocks, whether or not they're in its attack range and whichever side they're on, and attacks them first — the only exception is Marksmen: with a flying enemy in attack range they hit the flying enemy first (trait "attacks aerial enemies first", the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-08, GitHub #220) (except Medics: Medics other than Incantation Medics keep healing teammates while blocking and don't attack the blocked enemy; also except during "stop attacking" skills; skills whose original text has other restrictions follow that text: during Provence's S2 she doesn't target enemies above 80% HP, and the icicles of Santalla's S2 land on random tiles within her attack range).
- At battle start Operators deploy **column by column from left to right, each column top to bottom** (the leftmost column first, the same order as the Ægir devour's "further left and higher"), with summons after all Operators. Blocked enemies hit the Operator blocking them first; unblocked ranged enemies hit the highest Taunt level first, and among equals the most recently deployed (summons, lower Operators in the rightmost column, and freshly redeployed Operators get hit more easily). Invisible Operators are only attacked by enemies they block; splash, explosions, area skills and lingering damage zones from enemies they aren't blocking (including the toxic mist an OpFor: Erosion sprays when knocked out), and the attack-speed-reducing auras of the Dublinn Companion Guard and Zaro also don't land on them (as in the official game; effects that explicitly "ignore untargetable", such as Contamination Corrosion, still hit, and Frost's attack-speed-reducing aura and map effects like 'Gravestone' still apply); **Camouflage** (such as after Vulpisfoglia knocks out an enemy during her S3, or Kroos the Keen Glint's S1) is likewise only attacked by the enemies it blocks and is also shown semi-transparent, but does not avoid splash, explosions or area damage (normal attacks that hit every unit in an area at once — like 'Armor Cleaver', 'Armor Crusher' and Sudaram — can't hit it) and doesn't count as Invisible (it doesn't trigger Siracusa and other "while Invisible" effects). Siracusa's Invisible ends on a timer (32 seconds + 0.4 seconds per Stack); Vulpisfoglia's Camouflage, per the official game, lasts until her next skill activation. **While Tippi is airborne** she only blocks flying enemies and ground enemies don't target her (normal attacks, splash, skill areas, burning zones and debuffs that need a target don't land on her); flying and hovering enemies, and effects that explicitly "ignore untargetable" (such as Contamination Corrosion), still hit her; ground enemies' auras and field-wide effects (such as 'Gravestone' halving ATK and DEF across the field) and counters triggered when she attacks enemies (such as the Heavy Scout's exposure) still apply, and damage over time applied before take-off keeps ticking.
- **Skills fire automatically** according to the official "skill strategy": typical manual skills fire when ready, about to attack / heal, and with an enemy in the initial attack range (healing skills: an injured teammate); skills **with their own skill range** (such as Texas's "Sword Rain", SilverAsh the Reignfrost's "Countergambit", Degenbrecher's "Return To Silence", excluding "Attack Range expands"-type skills) fire as soon as **an enemy appears in the skill range**, without attacking first; manual skills **whose attack range is larger while active** (such as Mostima's "Key of Chronology", SilverAsh's "Truesilver Slash", Surtr's "Twilight", Ptilopsis's "Enkephalin") fire as soon as **an enemy appears in the attack range they would have when active** (healing skills: an injured teammate), without waiting for enemies to walk into the normal attack range — an intentional difference from the official game made per player feedback; **Defenders'** manual skills fire on taking damage (exceptions: Underflow's and Liskarm's S2, Horn's S2 and S3, and Ashlock's S1 and S2 were changed per player feedback to fire whenever an enemy is in attack range (Underflow's S2 judged by its longer active attack range), and Yu's S2 "For Guests, Bounteous Gifts" was changed to fire whenever an enemy is within two tiles around him (the skill range) — this game intentionally differs from the official one here; Underflow's S1 still requires taking damage); Standard Bearers / Tacticians / Bards fire as soon as the skill is ready, and Liberators / Phalanx Casters fire when an enemy is in the initial attack range (skills whose attack range expands when active — Mint's "Wind Whispers", Beeswax's "Growing Sandstorm", Carnelian's "Mark of Gluttony", Młynar's "Unresolved Sorrow", and Angelina's "Arcane Staff - Anti-Gravity Mode" — are likewise judged by the active attack range). **Auto-trigger** skills follow their own rules: those that only act on teammates (such as Thorns the Lodestar's "Wave Reckoning", Skadi the Corrupting Heart's "Wish of Burial Beyond the Light", Eyjafjalla the Hvít Aska's "Soundless Sustenance") fire as soon as their SP is full, without waiting for enemies or the injured; skills that take effect immediately and aren't an attack themselves (such as Wave Reckoning and Vanguards' DP skills) fire at least one attack interval apart (firing doesn't use up a normal attack), so faster attack speed fires them more often, and however much SP Swift returns they never fire every frame (GitHub #298); Vigil's "Packleader's Call" and "Packleader's Gift" only fire while the Wolfpack is on the field: while the Wolfpack is in tactical-point form, "Packleader's Call" fires too and brings it straight back onto the field with 1 wolf shadow (adding 1 wolf shadow when not full, and fully healing the Wolfpack when full), and "Packleader's Gift" fires too (gaining the DP immediately and saving the empowerment for the returning Wolfpack's next attack); or e.g. Gummy's "Provisions" fires when an injured teammate is nearby (within skill range), turning her next attack into a heal on that teammate (no need to engage first; her S2 "Cooking" is a manual skill and fires on taking damage per the Defender rule); Saria's "First Aid" only fires when she's about to attack and a teammate at or below half HP is nearby, and that attack becomes a heal instead; the shield from Exusiai the New Covenant's S2, per the official game, goes to the teammate with the **highest aggro** in attack range (higher Taunt level first, and among equals the most recently deployed), which isn't necessarily the one at the front taking hits. Automatic firing of manual skills has a 3-second cooldown: after deploying at battle start it first waits 3 seconds, and afterwards the same Operator's automatic activations are at least 3 seconds apart (skills with charges won't dump all charges at once). Stainless's "Prototype of Feist's Metal Crab" can be attacked by our Operators (taking no damage): while it is in an attack range / skill range, skills that can act on it fire automatically as if it were an enemy and spend ammo (confirmed by the project maintainer on 2026-10-08 per the official experience; the Stainless™ Multifunctional Platform can't be attacked); skills whose effect only picks enemies and can't hit it (such as Texas's "Sword Rain") don't fire for it, saving themselves for real enemies.
- **Push / pull** distance is set by the official "force − weight": e.g. Wild Mane's S2 (medium force) pushes enemies of weight 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 about 2.1 / 1.7 / 0.4 / 0.1 tiles, and can't move weight 4 or above — lighter enemies get pushed further. Wild Mane's S2 pushes along her facing: within 45° straight ahead the distances above apply, while enemies behind, beside or right against her only get a small nudge (within about 0.4 tiles); the coins of Swire the Elegant Wit's S3 instead push outward from her and land on ground enemies within 2–4 tiles in front of her (official "range 2-4") and on the enemies she blocks. Pushed and pulled enemies go into the official **Unbalance** state, during which they don't walk or normal-attack: a push lasts 0.2 to about 1.17 seconds depending on force minus weight (0.8 seconds for medium force pushing a weight-1 enemy), and a pull lasts 1 second (0.5 seconds when the force is 2 levels below the weight, and the same once pulled up close); during Unbalance enemies also don't use skills (such as Crownslayer's blink) or scripted moves (such as the Seed of Withering's dive), and Arc Frontliners take 400 true damage every 0.066 seconds during it; aerial units too heavy to push (static rigid bodies) pause for 0.1 seconds when pushed and for the whole pull duration when pulled; levitation, rebirth and teleporting on the route all end Unbalance immediately. The slide on screen uses the same time (half that in real time at the default 2× speed). Attack speed bottoms out at 20 (it won't get slower even with fully stacked slows from swamps, Frost, etc.).
- **HP regeneration is not healing** (as in the official game): the "restores HP per second" given to allies by the Bard trait (Civilight Eterna, Skadi the Corrupting Heart and her Seaborn), Perfumer's talent, Blemishine's S2, Suzuran's S3, and the alchemy units of Tin Man and Thorns the Lodestar (S1 and S2) raises their **HP regeneration rate**; it is not healing: Reapers / Juggernauts / Soloblades (such as Entelechia, Executor the Ex Foedere, Vetochki) that "can't be healed by allies" and Operators under heal prevention also get it, and healing bonuses (such as "healing received increased") don't amplify it; there is no regeneration during the first second after Suzuran's S3 activates. Direct healing (Medics' heals, skills that "restore … HP") still doesn't work on these Operators.
- The **Elemental Damage bar** sits right under the unit's own HP bar: the element icon on the left, and the white bar is the remaining element value, which only runs out on an actual burst; after bursting the bar turns the element's color and the icon flashes, slowly refilling over the cooldown (no Elemental Damage accumulates during that time).
- **Ammo, wolf shadows and "Self-Attachment"** are drawn on the HP bar too (0.2.2): while an ammo skill (such as Insider's S2, 14 rounds) is active, the SP bar under the HP bar becomes a **segmented yellow ammo bar** — one segment per round, losing one per shot and coming back on reload; during these skills there is no longer a constant gold aura under their feet. Vigil's Wolfpack has a row of **diamonds** under its HP bar: lit ones are the remaining wolf shadows, dim ones the used-up slots (at most 3), and they disappear in tactical-point form. When Hoshiguma the Breacher enters "Self-Attachment", her green HP bar stops at 1 and the excess damage grows as a **red bar** on the HP bar; she only falls when the red bar is full. These are display only and don't change any battle numbers.
- **Enemies**: per the official game, ranged enemies' range is measured from the Operator's collision body (radius 0.25 tiles), reaching 0.25 tiles further than the attack-range radius in the data — e.g. the Sarkaz Wither Tank (range 2.2) can hit Operators on ground tiles within 2.45 tiles of its center. Unblocked ranged enemies stop for each attack and finish the attack animation, only moving on between attacks. Its first attack is always a "corrosion bombardment" (leaving a 1.7-radius contamination zone at the target for 10 seconds: 50 true damage per second on ground tiles, 25 on ranged tiles, same-name zones don't stack), and its normal attack damage doubles while blocked. Enemies such as 'Wings of the Sankta' fly around randomly in a small area in place for 5 seconds the first time their HP drops below half (officially called "Fear"); Fear inflicted by Operators such as Siracusa and Nymph makes enemies flee away from the Operator. Invisible enemies can only be targeted while blocked (or revealed), and splash and circular skill-area damage can't hit unblocked invisible enemies (as in the official game); after a block ends they can still be targeted for 3 seconds (enemies whose official page says 0 or 1 second follow the page) before turning invisible again: each time a Dublinn Flamechaser Soldier is knocked out it first takes 1 second to rebirth, then becomes an invisible "Remnant Ash" that keeps walking (it takes 5 hits to knock out, 10 for the escort's ash), which usually has to be blocked by an Operator to be targeted (it stops being semi-transparent while blocked; if it was knocked out while blocked, it can also be finished off with ranged or area damage for about 2 seconds after the rebirth ends), and stands back up at full HP if not cleared within 10 seconds; on rebirth an enemy's slow, fear and other statuses are cleared and its skill cooldowns start over, and a feared Flamechaser that turns to ash can still be blocked; Decode Basis α nullifies all damage in its original form, and on its 4th instance of physical damage, 4th instance of arts damage, or when blocked, spends 2 seconds turning into an Avenger, a Special Tactician or a Phantom, and only then can it be knocked out.
- **Revival** (the first 3 from the "Ermengarde" Strategy, the M3 Cocoon Shell) works as in the official game: it counts as a knockout, immediately followed by a free instant redeploy where the Operator fell — both the Operator's "when knocked out" and "when deployed" effects trigger (e.g. Gravel's Resilient / Kazimierz Stacks, Blemishine's Stacks, the Kazimierz Alliance's deploy ATK), and SP and buffs reset as on a normal redeploy.
- **The "Duck Lord" Strategy ('Mystery Customer')**: from round 5 on, every battle of yours and your teammates' (**Final Assault and Hidden Core included**; Unite doesn't do a separate replacement) has 0–2 ground enemies (excluding Leaders and their parts, and Bounty enemies) replaced by special versions of Duck Lord, Gopnik, Crying Thief or Fatty, appearing at the replaced enemy's time and on its route (in two-player Leader fights, each player only has the enemies heading for their own protection objective replaced); whoever knocks one out gets 1 Fund (teammates during Unite count too), and one that leaks into the protection objective costs 1 LP as usual. Duck Lord and Fatty **don't attack and can't be blocked**: Duck Lord gets +300% movement speed after taking damage; Fatty always faces the side with more of our Operators and takes −80% physical / arts damage from the front. Crying Thief is Invisible (can only be targeted while blocked), and its attacks stun the target for 3.5 seconds, and a stunned Operator can't block it (official rule); after being released it takes 1 second to turn invisible again (0 seconds officially; this mode loosens it on purpose, the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-07); Gopnik is immune to Stun, Freeze and Levitate.
- 🔍▶▶ in the top-right corner shows the enemies coming this round (preview area), and 🔍 in the top-left shows the match info (disabled Alliances, disabled Operators, Strategies).

## 5. Unite (Team)

Triggers in a round when **someone leaks enemies and someone else has a perfect battle** (wiped every enemy with no leaks): up to 2 teammates with perfect battles bring their formations (as in the official game, only the HP ratio and SP from the end of their battle are kept: skills still active at the end of the battle start Unite turned off, with the SP left after activation, usually 0; summons keep only their SP; Operators already knocked out at the end of the battle lie in place (the Ægir opening devour still counts them as standing Operators for its marks and base ATK and Block, after which they leave as usual) and, per the official "forced retreat", restart a full redeploy countdown, redeploying as usual once time is up and there's enough DP — if Unite is short they may not make it back in time; start-of-battle redeploy-time reductions such as the "Draft" Improv also apply) to intercept all the enemies the other players leaked. Alliance Stacks are counted as reached at the end of your own battle this round (the Stacks shown in the Alliance bar), and don't stack further during Unite; these Stacks are counted only once at Settlement.

- Participants are chosen by the official rules: most units on the field > has active Alliances > most units still standing > earlier seat (LP doesn't matter); with two participants, the higher-ranked one (then comparing the total Stacks of active Alliances) "engages first" on the right half of the field.
- Unite takes place on **this round's battlefield** (as in the official game: the terrain, water, boxes and devices all stay), with both the left and right halves in play; Operators stand where they were placed in the Rest Phase, and with two participants the higher-ranked one moves 8 tiles right as a whole, onto the right half. With one participant, enemies enter the left half from the middle entrance; with two, enemies enter from the far right, pass through the right half first and then cross the middle into the left half. Enemies pushed back may cross the middle entrance into the right half and later walk back along their route. (0.2.0 had moved Unite onto one big empty road; from 0.2.1 it is back on this round's battlefield.)

- Only teammates with perfect battles can take part in Unite; **Alliances don't gain Stacks** during Unite.
- The Unite result is **only reported in the "Battle over" box at Settlement** (as in the official game, there are no "Unite succeeded / failed" captions). The number in the box is what the server **actually deducted from you** at Settlement: the player who leaked loses LP by the enemies left after Unite, not by how many leaked in their own battle. **"All unharmed!" only appears when Unite let nothing through** (nobody lost anything this round); when Unite didn't hold but you lost nothing (you were a helping teammate, or the enemies you leaked were all stopped), the box only has the "Battle over" title — the teammate who lost LP wasn't unharmed, and the official dialog has no other line either. Box color: cyan = Unite stopped everything, orange = Unite didn't hold but you lost no LP, red = you lost LP. How many you leaked is still shown by the orange "×N" in the top bar.
- Bounty enemies killed during Unite pay Funds to the killer; when no Operator knocked it out (source-less damage, its own HP loss, damage from other enemies, etc.), the Funds go to the teammate on whose half it fell. A Bounty enemy burned to death by terrain (such as Active Originium) on your own battlefield still pays you.
- Enemies still alive after Unite cost their original owner LP; when nobody had a perfect battle the LP is deducted directly.
- The player who leaked sees an orange "×N" tag in the middle of the top bar (next to the knockout count): the number of enemies you leaked that are still on the Unite battlefield (including those not yet spawned, **no cap**), decreasing by one each time a teammate knocks one out (when an enemy that splits or summons is knocked out, the spawned enemies count as yours too, and the number goes up accordingly); LP is simultaneously shown with min(N, 10) deducted. The leaking teammate's row in the team list on the left also shows ×N (updated in sync with the knockouts on the battlefield; hover to see whose leaked enemies they are).
- **A sound plays whenever an enemy enters a blue gate**, without waiting for it to actually cost LP — the part beyond the 10-per-round cap, enemies the data marks as not counted (Boss parts and the like), and the ones that leaked in Unite and were then cleared by teammates don't cost any more LP, but the sound still plays. It is **the sound the original stages play when an enemy enters a blue gate** (`ON_ENEMY_REACHED_EXIT` in the official sound table, the same in every stage). You hear the battlefield you're watching, so you also hear it when viewing a teammate's battlefield or watching Unite after leaking. The sound is 1.44 seconds long, and following the official "only one at a time" we make it **one shared sound per 1.5 seconds**: a wave breaking through plays it only once (not a pile-up), and leaking again a while later plays it again.
- Each normal round costs each player **at most 10 LP**.

## 6. Alliances

Each Operator belongs to 1–2 Alliances. An Alliance activates once enough **distinct** members are on the field (most only count the board; "Foresight", "Marvel" and "Investor" also count the Bench).

When the Alliance bar covers the board, click "Collapse" on the left to hide the list, and click "Alliance" to expand it again. Collapsing closes the details opened from the Alliance bar, and the spots the icons covered can be tapped or dragged directly; the collapsed state is kept when rounds change and when viewing teammates during the match.

- **Core Alliances** (8 factions: Yan, Sargon, Victoria, Kjerag, Laterano, Ægir, Siracusa, Kazimierz): activate at 3 members, with a powerful extra effect at 6.
- **Add-on Alliances** (Precision, Swift, Agile, Arcane, Durable, Aid, Foresight, Marvel, Investor, Raid, Resilient, Harmony, Assist Operator, Solo, Elite): most activate at 2 / 3 members; "Solo" needs **exactly 1** on the field, the "Elite" Alliance counts the number of Elites on the field, and Operators without a faction Alliance count as "Assist Operator".
- **Disabled this match**: "Standard Simulation", as in the official game, turns off these 10 Alliances: Laterano, Ægir, Kazimierz, Agile, Arcane, Marvel, Investor, Raid, Solo and Elite; they never activate. When you have members of these Alliances (Bench members count for Marvel and Investor), the end of the Alliance bar shows **grey Alliance icons with an ✕ and "Disabled this match"**, and Operator cards, Alliance tags and Alliance details are marked the same way. "Foresight", "Marvel" and "Investor" count the Bench, and the "Members" count in the Alliance details counts Bench members the same way (the Temporary Bench doesn't count).
- **Harmony**: when active, each Core Alliance that already has members on the field gets +1 to its count (just +1, no matter how many Harmony Operators there are). The Alliance details say "incl. Harmony +1", and the Harmony Operator providing that 1 (e.g. Muelsyse) is listed above the member list.
- **Yan's Protection**: with 6 or more "Yan" Operators on the field it is summoned (two with 9). It tracks the enemy with the highest aggro on the field and hovers above it; its skill "Flame of Exorcism" locks onto 1 target and **breathes fire continuously** (up to 20 seconds, dealing arts damage per second to enemies around the target and applying Burn; it doesn't normal-attack while breathing fire, and stops when the target dies or it's Silenced); when there are temporarily no enemies on the field it waits in place. It has the official "Isolated": Medics won't heal it, and allied buffs, auras and talents won't select it (enemies can still attack it). It is a flying unit: enemy effects marked "can't hit aerial" (the death explosions of the Infused Originium Slug / Infused Glacial Originium Slug / Cabbage Seedling, the Suiton Ninja's vortex, Boiling Pot, the prayer invitation of 'Sankta Head Held High', the attacks of 'Armor Cleaver' / 'Armor Crusher', etc.) don't reach it.
- **Stacks**: Alliances gain Stacks through various effects, and more Stacks mean stronger effects (e.g. "Yan" ATK increases with Stacks, "Foresight" gives Funds every 10 Stacks). **Stacks persist for the whole match but only take effect while the Alliance is active**, and in most cases only active Alliances can gain Stacks. Each Alliance has at most **999 Stacks** (same as the official game, taken from the official client's constant): once at 999, further Stacks are simply discarded, and Stack-based rewards (such as "Foresight"'s Funds) stop too. Stacks gained during battle are shown **live** in the Alliance bar, and are kept after the battle until Settlement (they don't roll back during Unite either). "Harmony", "Assist Operator", "Solo" and "Elite", as in the official game, **don't show Stacks** (not in the Alliance bar, the details or the result cards), but the Attributes / Strategies / Equipment that add Stacks still add Stacks to them (PRTS Alliance notes: "some Alliances don't display their Stacks, but Attribute / Strategy / Equipment effects that add Stacks still apply to them"), and these Stacks also count toward the total active Stacks — only the Strategy "Yu"'s 【Slow Simmer】 doesn't add Stacks to these four Alliances (in round 8, if only one of them is active, it adds nothing; the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-08); their effects themselves don't depend on Stacks.
- **Where Attributes take effect**: "on entering the Rest Phase", "at the end of the Rest Phase" and "on Refresh" Attributes (the first item on the detail card) only take effect while the Operator is deployed in the battle area; Lappland, Perfumer and Podenco, whose text says "also effective while this Operator is on the Bench", and Swire the Elegant Wit with 【Yan】 / 【Investor】 active, also work on the Bench (not the Temporary Bench), matching the official data; "on obtaining" and "on selling" Attributes take effect at the moment of obtaining or selling, wherever the Operator is. The "Operators on the Bench can also be used to activate the Alliance" of "Foresight", "Marvel" and "Investor" only counts Bench members toward the number; it doesn't make Attributes such as Silence's work on the Bench. Attributes that say "make the active …" require the Alliance to be active at that moment: e.g. "Siracusa" only counts distinct Siracusa Operators in the battle area, so a Lappland on the Bench isn't one of the 3; Lappland only stacks on the first manual Refresh she goes through each round (free Refreshes count too), and if Siracusa wasn't active at that Refresh, she won't stack again that round.
- **How bonuses stack**: "ATK / DEF / HP +X%" from Alliances, Strategies, Equipment, Improv cards and Attributes is **added** to a skill's "ATK +X%" and then multiplied onto the stat (official: direct multiplication), e.g. Precision +130%, Solo +60% and a +40% Equipment piece total +230%, not ×2.3×1.6×1.4. Damage multipliers like "damage increased to X%" or "damage taken +X%" (Victoria, Kjerag, Arcane, Fragile, etc.) still multiply with each other; but same-name effects count only once — when both players on one battlefield have "Arcane" active, enemies get only one layer of Arcane vulnerability, taking the higher of the two (no longer multiplied); two "Gnosis" Rigid Ice effects, or the RES reduction from two "Leonhardt" / "Texas the Omertosa", likewise count only the strongest one. "Durable"'s reflected damage is credited to the member being hit (visible in the per-player Leader damage stats).
- Each match randomly **disables** a number of Alliances (fewer in Standard Simulation), and their Operators won't appear in the shop; "Confirm Match Info" at the start lists them, and you can review them again while choosing a Strategy ("View disabled Alliances & Operators") and during the match (🔍 top-left).
- Two pieces of the same Equipment merge into a stronger piece. A piece that only arrives at the end of the Rest Phase (as with the Victorian Hammer Victoria gives every 25 Stacks) first stays on the Bench, an equipped piece isn't taken off for this battle, and the merged advanced piece appears **at the start of the next Rest Phase**; same-name pieces obtained at other times still merge immediately. When both the Bench and the Temporary Bench are full, the new piece is destroyed as before ("Bench is full; the obtained Equipment was destroyed"), and isn't swapped into an Equipment slot. Certain Equipment combinations can grant an Operator extra Alliance effects: when the "Damazti Isomorph" is carried together with another Alliance Equipment (such as a Victorian Hammer), the holder counts as a member of the Alliance that Equipment corresponds to. The mapping is shown in the "Talent" section of the Damazti Isomorph's detail card (Alliances disabled this match are marked; when equipped on an Operator, the active group is highlighted).

## 7. Final Assault and Hidden Core

- **Final Assault** (round 14; round 9 for Solo Standard): players are paired by seat, and each pair shares one battlefield, one defending the left half and one the right half (the right-hand player's board is mirrored left-to-right). Everyone **whittles down the same Leader HP bar together** (HP = the official Leader HP for that difficulty × the number of players alive at the start of the battle: the Leader HP comes from the current version's game data, e.g. Ultimate "OpFor: Armor" 3 600 000, which is 14 400 000 with 4 players alive and 7 200 000 with 2; AI teammates and disconnected players on autopilot count, eliminated players and those who left don't; Solo Simulation counts as 1. This is the author's decision of 2026-10-06 adopting community PR #209, replacing the earlier "fixed HP"), and you can't see the other pair's battlefield. The whole team's LP is merged into one pool, with no more 10-per-round cap. When the 120-second countdown at the top ends, the battle **continues** (no loss on timeout); from second 150 of the battle (real seconds) the whole team loses 1 LP per second. The Leader's HP reaching zero is a win (when the bar shows 0 the Leader has fallen and the battle ends immediately), and LP reaching zero is a loss — whichever hits zero first counts, and damage dealt after LP runs out no longer counts. Alliances don't gain Stacks in this phase.
- **Damage cap** (Final Assault and Hidden Core, same as the official game): **any single hit of 300000 or more on the Leader is voided entirely** — that hit deals 0 damage, doesn't count toward the Leader HP bar and shows no damage number; it isn't clamped to 300000, it counts for nothing at all (299999 works as normal). This only applies to the Leader itself (including the other Leader on a two-player battlefield); the Leader's parts, escorts, drones and other minions aren't affected, nor are normal rounds or Unite; when a part transfers damage 1:1 to the Leader, that is also checked as a single hit; the 2% of the bar the Leader loses when a drone dies doesn't count as a hit and isn't subject to the damage cap (in the Hidden Core on Ultimate, "OpFor: Armor" with 3 or 4 players alive is 432 000 / 576 000 per drone, which the damage cap would void entirely; this game still counts it — an inference). With extremely high Stacks the Leader can still fall within seconds (e.g. both of each player's Alliances at 999 Stacks, or all active Alliances at 999 Stacks); this comes from the official uncapped Stack-based damage multipliers (Kjerag, Arcane, Precision, etc.), and the official community also talks about "boss dies in one second".
- In the Final Assault / Hidden Core Rest Phase, as in battle, **the two players of a pair are on the same final battlefield**: you place on your own half (the right-hand player sees themselves on the right, with placement mirrored left-to-right and the Operators' facing relative to the Leader unchanged), and your teammate's current formation is shown on the other half (view-only, updating live as they adjust it); in the member list on the left the avatars of the two players in a pair have a **green frame**, from the start of the round until the end of the battle. A spectator viewing either of them also sees both formations and the Leader standing at the end of the field.
- In the Final Assault / Hidden Core Rest Phase, deployable tiles are determined by **your half of the final battlefield** (mirrored for the right-hand player), and the tiles highlighted while dragging are where you can place: e.g. on the "Originium Flow Generator" final map, the tiles enclosed by the 1×3 orange pipes can be deployed on. Operators placed on these tiles in the Final Assault stay in place when entering the Hidden Core.
- **Leader parts**: when one of "OpFor: Armor"'s drones dies (no matter who downs it; leaking doesn't count), Armor loses 2% of the Leader HP bar's total (e.g. Ultimate with 4 players alive 14 400 000 → 288 000 per drone; the official text doesn't specify which "max HP" this refers to, and this game uses the bar's total — an inference); every point of damage taken by the Hidden Core's "Armor Cleaver / Armor Crusher" and "Gun-Breaker Spring" is deducted 1:1 from the Leader HP bar (always counts after the Cleaver / Crusher has been knocked down; hits taken mid-dive also count — the two official texts disagree on this point, so it's an inference). The Cleaver / Crusher normally hovers next to Armor, invulnerable; when thrown it blinks to a fixed route and charges the Operator with the lowest ATK, and after being hit 15 times along the way it is 【Paralyzed】 (stunned for 10 seconds, grounded, damage taken ×1.3); after landing or when the paralysis ends, it is replaced with a new one carrying its remaining HP back at its original position. "Catastrophe" fires only 1 Armor Piercer each time (the same below 20% HP), and only hits the Operator with the highest ATK in its attack range.
- **Huge Leaders** (OpFor: Armor / Pipe, Sal Viento Bishop Quintus, Alistair, 'Samivilinn,' the Resolution of the Very North) have a hit area of about 5×3 tiles (the whole pipe block): an attack range touching any one of those tiles can hit them; they are **self-bound and can't be blocked**, and never leave their spot. Splash is judged from the enemy's center, so splash landing on nearby minions doesn't reach a huge Leader.
- **Hidden Core** (round 15, Perilous and above only): after defeating the Leader, if the team's total Stacks across active Alliances is **> 1200** (Solo > 350) and LP > 1, you enter the Hidden Core to challenge a stronger Leader. Failing it doesn't affect clearing the match.

## 8. Emotes and spectating

- "Chat" in the bottom-left opens the emote panel: 6 sets × 6 emotes; swipe left / right (or use the arrow keys) to switch sets. Emotes show as a bubble next to your avatar for 3 seconds, with a 1-second send cooldown.
- During the Rest Phase, or after your own battle ends (showing "Battle over, waiting for teammates to finish"), click a teammate's avatar on the left → "Go View" to watch their battlefield; you can't spectate while your own battle is in progress. During Unite and the Final Assault you can switch between left half / Overview / right half with ‹ ›.
- After being eliminated you can freely watch any battlefield, and **you don't have to choose again every round**: whoever you last chose with "Go View", every later phase (round start, Improv, Rest Phase, battle, Final Assault) follows their field or battlefield automatically; if you never chose or they were eliminated too, it follows the first Doctor still in the match in seat order (AI teammates included). Switching mid-phase takes effect immediately and it follows the newly chosen player from then on.
- **Spectator seats** (new in this remake, not in the original): every alliance room has 2 extra spectator seats. Enter the Alliance Key in the lobby and click "Spectate" to join (also while a simulation is running); spectators don't take a Doctor seat or count as players, and can't buy, deploy, ready up or send emotes; they watch the same way eliminated players do — automatically following the Doctor they last viewed (if none, the first Doctor still in the match in seat order): that Doctor's field in the Rest Phase and their battlefield in battle, and tapping a member avatar on the left switches to anyone. Reconnecting returns you to your spectator seat; the host can remove a spectator in the room with the ✕ next to their name; once the simulation starts, a small "👁 count" capsule appears next to the ping in the top bar (not shown when there are no spectators), which opens the spectator list (offline ones are marked), where the host can remove a spectator with ✕ — the removed player goes straight back to the lobby and stops receiving the battlefield (there are still at most 2 spectator seats; this can't be changed); a spectator can click "Take a Seat" to become a Doctor when the room has a free seat.
- **Whoever you watch, the Alliance bar shows their Alliances**: when viewing a teammate's field or battlefield (during the Rest Phase, after the battle, or auto-spectating after elimination), the Alliance bar at the top left switches to **that teammate's Alliances, counts and Stacks**, prefixed with an amber "👁 teammate name" tag, and the Alliance details you open also say "teammate name's Alliance" (the members column marks their Operators on the field; when viewing during the Rest Phase their Operators on the Bench and Temporary Bench are listed too, marked "Bench", and counted the same way they see it: Alliances that include the Bench, such as Investor, Foresight and Marvel, count Bench members toward the number, and the Temporary Bench counts for no Alliance; during battle only those on the field are listed); during battle the Stacks grow live (matching what they see themselves). In Unite and the Final Assault it follows ‹ ›: with the camera on the teammate's half it shows their Alliances, and on your own half it shows yours; in "Overview", if you're fighting on that battlefield it shows yours, and if you're not on it (watching Unite after leaking, spectating after elimination) it shows the teammate you chose with "Go View", or, if you never chose one, the first player on that battlefield (Unite participant no. 1 / the first player of that pair) — **never your own**. On such a battlefield, clicking a teammate's avatar → "Go View" moves the camera straight to their half, and the "👁 teammate name" on ‹ › matches the Alliance bar's tag; next to ‹ › there's "Return to Battlefield" (not shown when you're eliminated). When the Alliance bar switches person, any Alliance details opened from it close automatically. When you open the detail card of an Operator on the field, the Alliances on the card (and the Alliance details opened from it) belong to **that Operator's owner**, regardless of whom the Alliance bar is currently showing — e.g. clicking a teammate's Operator in "Overview" shows "teammate name's Alliance" with their counts and Stacks; clicking your own Operator on a teammate's half shows your own. Going back to your own field / battlefield restores your own Alliances. If you refresh the page / reconnect while watching a teammate after the battle, you return to the teammate's battlefield you were watching, with "👁 teammate name" and "Return to Battlefield" still there. Eliminated teammates no longer show Alliances (and can't be viewed).

## 9. Result titles

At Settlement titles are awarded based on performance (at most one per player, and each title goes to only one player): **Stronghold Star** (highest Leader damage, only on a win), **Steadfast Alliance** (highest total active Stacks), **Rock-Solid Defense** (least LP lost), **Elites Assemble** (most Elite promotions), **Fully Armed** (most Equipment assigned), **Big Spender** (most Funds spent).

## 10. Tips

- Early on, prioritize assembling one Alliance you can activate — it's steadier than buying scattered high-tier Operators; 3 copies of the same Operator promote automatically and give a free high-tier recruit.
- Funds don't carry over to the next round: if you haven't used them, Refresh or upgrade; when you can't afford a card you want, **Freeze** first.
- The upgrade price drops by 1 each round automatically, so if you're not in a hurry you can wait a round or two before upgrading.
- During the Rest Phase check the enemy preview at the top right; if there are flying units, keep anti-air (ranged) Operators ready; **low-altitude hovering** enemies (Skimming Sea Drifter, Lucky Flyfin) also count as aerial units, so melee Operators and Operators that only hit ground units (such as Rosmontis) can't hit them, and they only drop down after being stunned, frozen or put to sleep (a Skimming Sea Drifter that has dropped keeps crawling, and its model switches to a crawling pose).
- In Team Simulation, don't compete with teammates for the same line of Operators (the pool is shared); splitting up to build different Alliances is easier.
- When you have only about 10 LP left (at most 10 can be lost per round), prioritize securing perfect battles.
- Unite only lets teammates with perfect battles join, so a formation that reliably clears enemies is valuable for the whole team.
- Entering the Hidden Core depends on the team's **total active Stacks**: in the late game you can stack Stacks deliberately.
- Stacks cap at 999, so excess stacking is useless — move your resources to other Alliances.
- Watch out for the **damage cap** in Leader fights: any single hit of 300,000 or more is voided entirely. With Alliances at 999 Stacks, stacking on more damage multipliers (e.g. lighting up Arcane on top of 999-Stack Kjerag) can actually make your big skills deal 0 damage; teammates' debuffs such as Arcane also amplify your damage.
- **Tiles enclosed by an orange fence** (official "wall") can be deployed on, and enemies can't walk in: Operators inside attack enemies in their attack range as usual (choose the facing when placing so the attack range covers the enemies' path), but **can't block ground enemies** (enemies pushed or pulled against the fence aren't blocked by it either), and rarely get hit — most Defenders' manual skills need incoming damage to fire, so inside the fence they may never fire (Underflow's S2 is the exception, firing whenever an enemy is in attack range); Operators that only hit the single tile under their feet (such as Matterhorn) can't hit any enemy from inside.

## 11. Hotkeys

With a keyboard you can use hotkeys (the table below shows the default keys). Hotkeys don't work while you're typing in an input field, or while a dialog or "How to Play" is open; while "Match Info / Enemy Intel" is open only `Esc` (closing it) works.

| Default key | Action |
|---|---|
| `R` | Refresh the shop |
| `F` | Freeze / unfreeze the shop |
| `D` | Upgrade the Dispatch Center |
| `Q` | Retreat the selected board Operator (back to the Bench) |
| `X` | Sell the selected Operator |
| `Space` | Ready / cancel Ready; pause / resume during a Solo Simulation battle |
| `Esc` | Cancel / close: closes, in order, the emote panel, the enemy preview, the Alliance overlay, the detail card, Match Info / Enemy Intel (fixed, can't be changed) |

Refresh, Freeze, Upgrade, Retreat, Sell and Ready only work in the Rest Phase, and Retreat / Sell need an Operator selected first. The direction wheel (arrow keys to preview, `Enter` to confirm, `Esc` to cancel; Tab first reaches "Tap to cancel" on the wheel, where `Enter` cancels, and `Enter` on the other buttons Tab reaches does nothing) and the page keys of "How to Play" and "Operator Loadout" are those screens' own keys and can't be changed.

**Changing hotkeys**: the "Settings" button on the title screen, "Settings" in the top bar of the lobby and the room (left of How to Play), or "Settings" in the bottom-left during a match → "Hotkeys". Click the key you want to change (keyboard only: Tab onto it and press Enter), and when "Press a key…" appears press the new key; `Esc` cancels without closing the Settings window. Letters, digits, Space, punctuation keys and Delete / Insert / Home / End / PageUp / PageDown can be used; `Esc`, `Tab`, `Enter`, the arrow keys and F1–F12 are reserved for the interface, and Ctrl / Alt / ⌘ combinations aren't allowed. If the new key is already used by another hotkey the two swap, as noted below the list (e.g. changing "Retreat selected Operator" to `X` moves "Sell selected Operator" to the old `Q`). "Reset to Default" restores the keys in the table above. Changes take effect immediately, and the keys shown on the shop bar, the Ready button and the units' bottom "Retreat / Sell" buttons follow; the setting is saved in this browser together with volume and the rest (it goes back to default in another browser or after clearing site data). Keys are recognized by the character printed on the keycap (AZERTY and other keyboards by their printed letters), and non-Latin layouts such as Russian by key position. Touch-only devices such as phones need a keyboard connected to use hotkeys, and the hotkey list in Settings is collapsed by default.

## 12. Reporting problems

When you hit an error, get stuck or see a wrong result, open "Settings" → "Report a problem", click "Copy diagnostics", then describe what happened in a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/sganggs/Stronghold-Protocol/issues) and paste it in. The diagnostics include the game version, the browser and device, the current phase and round, and the errors recorded since the page was opened (at most the latest 40). When copied during a battle, the data of that battle is attached by default so developers can reproduce it; turn off "Attach this battle" if you don't want that. The diagnostics are generated only in your browser and never uploaded automatically; your name, other players' names and the room code are replaced by placeholders like `<me>`, `<player2>` and `<room>`. When the clipboard isn't available (some in-app browsers), the content is shown in the text box below; select all and copy it by hand.

## 13. Stats (local)

"Stats" at the top right of the title screen and in the top bar of the lobby and the room opens the **Statistics** page: the records of the matches you've played on this device. The records live only in this browser (`localStorage`); they're never uploaded to the server, and the server keeps no match history; in another browser, on another device or after clearing site data, the records don't come along. To move them, use "Export" at the top of the page (a JSON file) and "Import" on the other device (merged record by record, skipping duplicates).

| Tab | Contents |
|---|---|
| Overview | Total matches, wins, win rate, times the Hidden Core was reached / cleared, furthest round, total time; one row per difficulty; how many times you got each of the six result titles |
| Strategies | How many matches each Strategy was used in, how many it cleared (only counted when you survived to the end and the team won) and its clear rate |
| Combat totals | The numbers on the result screen, such as enemies knocked out, Leader damage and damage dealt, added up over the matches that reached Settlement |
| Match history | One row per match, newest at the top (50 rows at a time, "Show more" for the rest); click a row to review that match's result screen (the latest 30 matches can be reviewed; older ones keep only a summary and can't be opened) |

**What counts as a match**: matches that reached the result screen, and matches left early ("Abandon Simulation") after clearing at least 1 round, count in all the stats above — as in the official game, leaving early counts as elimination, and the rounds cleared are the ones cleared when you left. **Matches left right away, before clearing the first round, aren't counted** (they don't count as a match and don't affect the win rate or Strategy clear rates), but they stay in "Match history", marked "Left early" and "Not counted". Leaving a match after being eliminated counts (you lost that match). These aren't recorded: spectating; matches ended by the server (server restart, alliance disbanded); matches you closed the page on and never came back to.

**Storage and privacy**: at most the latest 1000 matches are kept, and all records together stay under about 0.8 MB (the oldest are dropped first beyond that). Only the latest 30 matches keep the full lineups, Alliances and teammate information; older ones are compressed to a one-line summary of your own. Team match records contain **your teammates' callsigns**; they're stored on this device and also appear in files you export, and are never sent to anyone. To delete them, click "Clear" (it asks first), or clear this site's data. A save that can't be read (corrupted data) is set aside as a backup and recording starts over, without breaking the page; files exported by a newer version are refused on import, and aren't overwritten by an older version.
